Beyond Pure Acoustics: How the Kev-4B Pipeline Overcomes Standalone SER Hallucinations

A 122-clip cross-corpus empirical study across English and Japanese reveals that cognitive arbitration resolves acoustic ambiguities in natural speech (+32% in Japanese) while exposing the artificial exaggeration of theatrical datasets.

For decades, Speech Emotion Recognition (SER) systems have relied purely on acoustic frame representations extracted from audio encoders such as Emotion2Vec+ or WavLM. While these neural models excel on theatrical datasets featuring exaggerated, bombastic vocal deliveries, they experience severe failure modes when confronted with natural conversation, foreign cultural intonations, and corporate sarcasm.

On the Japanese JVNV emotional speech corpus, standalone Emotion2Vec+ collapsed to a mere 64.0% emotion accuracy, repeatedly misclassifying genuine happiness and somber sadness as startled alarm or disgust. By introducing Kev-4B Cognitive Arbitration—which primes a 4-billion-parameter foundation model with verbatim transcripts, physical pitch (F0), and energy metrics alongside acoustic priors—accuracy soared to 96.0% emotion and 100% sentiment accuracy.

Conversely, on theatrical English corpora like RAVDESS, actors were instructed to speak identical neutral lines like "Dogs are sitting by the door" with artificial fury. Standalone SER classifies these solely on acoustic decibels. Kev-4B's cognitive arbitrator detects the profound conflict between harmless text and screaming acoustics, accurately highlighting sarcasm risk and passive-aggression.
